## Travel to LAX

Your departure point is Newark, New Jersey—the undisputed armpit of America, operating with all the charm and infrastructure of a developing nation.

> **Fun Fact:** Geese fly upside down over New Jersey. Scientists determined this is because there is literally nothing down there worth crapping on.

I will be launching out of Idaho Falls and am slated to land before you. However, our travel equation has two glaring failure points:

    1. Newark is the flight dealy capital of the free world
    2. The Northwest in February is a frozen arctic wasteland where planes freeze to the tarmac.

Do not panic. Tactical redundancy has already been engineered into the timeline. Newark wasn't selected for its hospitality or scenic beaches; it was chosen because it has non-stop, high-frequency cattle cars flying directly to Los Angeles. More importantly, our transpacific bird doesn't wheels-up until late the following night. You have roughly a 36-hour buffer window to make the gate—meaning even if Newark completely melts down, you’d practically have time to walk here.

If the airlines decide to test your resolve, you’re covered:
- <b>Trip Protection:</b> You have Capital One coverage backing the movement (see Capital One Coverage section).
- <b>Flexible Ticketing:</b> Your fare allows rebooking onto the next available bird if yours cancels.
- <b>Bivouac Plan:</b> We are sharing a room. I should be at LAX well before you, but if my flight gets socked in by a blizzard, Mom is authorized on the reservation and can execute check-in solo.
- <b>Strategic Patience:</b> If your flight is canceled and you are rebooked for the next day, you will still be on schedule for the 8:00 PM departure to Bangkok. Do not accept a "shortcut" involving a red-eye from a different airport. The direct route is the only one that respects the geopolitical realities of Chinese airspace.

> 🚨 **Warning** <br> In the event you run into an emergency, consult the [Emergency Section](#emergency) immediately. <br><br> Two golden rules apply at all times: <br> 1. **DON'T PANIC**, and whatever you do, <br> 2. **Know where your towel is.** A towel is about the most massively useful thing an interstellar traveler can carry across the Pacific.

The exact same defensive logic went into my route: I have to transit Seattle, battle the purple-haired zombies at Sea-Tac, and try to preserve my sanity and testosterone levels before hitting California.

You survive Newark, I survive Seattle. We can compare the psychological scars over a drink at the hotel.

## The Coat Dilemma

You have two choices here: Coat, or No Coat.

I am strictly Team No Coat. My SOP is simple: leave the heavy winter gear in the vehicle, hold your breath, and suck up the negative 20-degree frostbite air for 45 seconds at a sprint between the parking lot and the terminal door. Why? Because winter parkas are massive, bulky, and an absolute pain in the ass to haul through four different airports. Once we hit Asia, you won't need that heavy down coat for a single second of the entire operation.

>**Fun Fact:** Southeast Asia only has two official seasons: Hot, and Surface of the Sun. Mother Nature paired the heat with roughly 2,000% humidity, meaning the air isn't something you breathe—it’s something you chew. The second you step out of the airport, your shirt will instantly fuse to your ribs, your back will turn into an uncontrolled swamp ecosystem, and you will understand why cold beer is treated as essential medical supplies.

My personal travel rig is simple: a hoodie, jeans, broken-in sneakers, and a polo shirt underneath.

Why the setup? The polo claims you have your life together, but the hoodie says you're ready to endure a flying cattle car across the globe to look at ancient temples that predate Christianity and eat food so aggressively spiced it burns on insertion and burns on extraction.

It’s practical, easily rolled into a carry-on once we cross the equator, and crucially, doesn't scream: *"Look at me, I'm a fresh, disoriented tourist—please pickpocket my wallet."*

Thailand is roughly 12 to 14 hours ahead of your body clock, which means your internal wiring will be completely inverted. If you do not actively manage this transition, your brain will try to go to sleep at 2:00 PM and wake you up at 3:00 AM wired on adrenaline.

The good news: our international flight out of LAX doesn’t leave until late evening, and we have the hotel room booked through the 19th. We are not checking out at 17:00 and wandering the Los Angeles sidewalks like homeless transients with our luggage. We stay locked inside the air-conditioned wire until we are ready to move.

<b>The Mission: Stay Awake Until 0400:</b>

On our staging night before departure, the standing order is simple: <b>do not go to bed early</b>. You must force yourself to stay awake until at least <b>0400</b>.

Yes, 0400. It is the only way to force your brain chemistry to recognize that our "yesterday" is actually tomorrow's "today." If you go to sleep at a normal hour, you will wake up in Los Angeles at 3:00 AM feeling like you just chugged a gallon of rocket fuel while simultaneously being hit by a tranquilizer dart. You will be wired, useless, and angry. We do not do that.

This tactical sleep deprivation accomplishes two critical objectives:

1. <b>Front-loads the Jet Lag Shift: </b>It immediately drags your circadian rhythm halfway across the Pacific before you even board the aircraft.
2. <b>Guarantees In-Flight Unconsciousness: </b>When you finally climb aboard that 17-hour flying aluminum tube, you won’t be tossing, turning, and staring at the flight tracker every fifteen minutes. Your body will be so thoroughly exhausted that you will collapse into sleep and knock out a massive chunk of the flight like a sack of concrete.

Consider this voluntary biological hazing. Embrace the suck for a few hours in the comfort of a hotel bed so you don't spend three days in Bangkok stumbling around like a concussed zombie.
