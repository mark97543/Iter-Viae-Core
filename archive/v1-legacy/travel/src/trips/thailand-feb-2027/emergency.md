## Emergencies

Well, if you’re reading this section, you’re either an overachieving proactive planner or the wheels have completely fallen off the wagon. Let’s assume the wheels are currently bouncing across an active tarmac.

This section covers the four horsemen of international air travel:

1. **International Incidents** (Diplomatic friction, minor detention, or catastrophic misunderstandings)
2. **Delays Over Six Hours** (Terminal purgatory)
3. **Flight Cancellations** (Stranded in the eastern hemisphere)
4. **Lost Baggage** (Living in the clothes currently welded to your skin)

Before we troubleshoot a single airline screw-up, burn this foundational rule into your memory: **Mom is not the problem.**

The airline canceling our connection in Taipei is the problem. The monsoon stalling ground operations is the problem. The French air traffic controllers striking out of sheer routine is the problem. Mom asking for the fourth time where the gate is or whether we have our passports is merely a civilian coping mechanism. Directing tactical frustration toward her will result in immediate court-martial conditions with zero air support.

### Standard Operating Rules for Cosmic Travel Disasters

1. **DON'T PANIC.**  
   Printed in large, friendly letters on the cover of the *Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy*, and for good reason. Panicking burns precious oxygen, alienates airline customer service personnel, and rarely summons an aircraft out of thin air.
2. **Deploy the Towel.**  
   A towel is about the most massively useful thing an interstellar traveler can carry. If stranded in a freezing terminal overnight, it is a blanket; in tropical humidity, it is a mop; wrapped around your head, it wards off noxious airport fumes; dampened at a sink, it provides essential field hygiene. Always know where your towel is.
3. **Never Tell Me the Odds.**  
   Yes, Han Solo was right. If the gate agent announces there is only a 3% chance the stand-by list clears tonight, do not stand there doing probability math. Let the Quartermaster work the counter while you secure charging ports and water bottles.
4. **Assume the Total Perspective Vortex is Active.**  
   Standing in an immigration queue behind 800 international travelers moving at three inches per hour can destroy a man's soul. It reveals your exact insignificance in the grand machinery of global aviation. Take a deep breath, accept that time is an illusion, and do not look at your watch.
5. **The Airline Gate Agent Holds Imperial Authority.**  
   Treat whoever is behind that keyboard like a sentient supercomputer holding the launch codes. Yelling accomplishes nothing; polite, dry exhaustion paired with strategic deference gets you rebooked onto a Singapore Airlines partner flight before the mob notices.

### International Incidents

This includes images, statues, and the currency (the King's face is on every Baht note and coin).
   - **Field Application:** If you drop a paper 100-Baht bill and it starts blowing away in the wind, DO NOT STEP ON IT TO STOP IT. Stepping on the King’s face is a fast-track ticket to a very unpleasant holding cell. Let it blow, or chase it down with your hands.
   - **Zero Opinions:** Do not discuss the monarchy. Do not joke about the monarchy. If the Royal Anthem plays at 08:00 or 18:00 in train stations or public parks, simply freeze, stand at attention, and wait until everyone else moves.

2. **The Royal Thai Police Protocol**

   - **The "Jai Yen" (Cool Heart) Rule:** In Thai culture, losing your temper or yelling (Jai Ron / Hot Heart) causes everyone to lose face. The louder you yell, the less anyone will help you, and the more expensive your problem becomes.
   - **Deploy Native Intelligence First:** If Fa is present, step two paces back, shut your mouth, and let her talk. She knows the nuances, the hierarchy, and the exact tone of voice that de-escalates a bureaucrat. You are just a friendly tourist who is slightly confused.

### Lost Baggage

Checked baggage going missing is an inconvenience, not a fatal mission error. Because our itinerary has two distinct flight stages, the fallout depends entirely on which airline misplaces the gear:

- **If Alaska Airlines loses it between Idaho Falls and LAX:** We have our 35-hour LAX staging cushion to let them route the bag to Los Angeles. If it still doesn't arrive before SQ 37 departs, we abandon the bag, board the plane, and buy cheap clothes in Thailand.
- **If Singapore Airlines loses it between LAX, Singapore, and Bangkok:** Singapore Airlines has exceptional baggage tracing. They will deliver the recovered luggage straight to our hotel lobby in Bangkok or Surin via local courier.

1. **The Pre-Emptive Carry-On Rule: The "72-Hour Survival Kit"**
